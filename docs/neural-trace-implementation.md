# Neural trace: implementation contract

The build plan for `docs/neural-trace.md`, `docs/neural-trace-states.md` and `docs/neural-trace-performance.md`. Those three are the design; this one fixes names, files, rules and gates so the pieces built in parallel fit. The reference behaviour is the browser mock: `docs/neural-trace-mocks/src/` (`engine.js` layout, edges, ties, ganglia and node cards; `hero2.js` states, the frame function, the shaders; `f.js` the conversation integration, replay, provenance and answer streaming; `data.js` the fixtures). A port starts from those files and adapts them to the real types.

## Decisions that are fixed

- One brain per turn. Everything a turn did before its final message is the brain: thinking, tool calls and the agent's earlier notes. The final answer is the trailing text after the last tool or reasoning part of that turn.
- The brain replaces the activity run rows ("N tools" lines, standalone thinking rows). The old rows stay as the **Lines** style (`ActivityStyleSetting`, key `tailscode.activityStyle`, default Brain) so a person, a GPU without ASTC and a shed budget can keep them.
- Metal on iOS and the Mac. A CPU delta compositor (pure Swift, in Core) on Linux, because an animating `GtkGLArea` leaks about 70 MB a minute on the vendor's EGL stack (`CGtkShim/shim.c`, the orb comment). The same compositor is the reference for the shader, the golden for the tests and the painter for share posters.
- 30 fps (`ActivityTuning.frameRate`, 24 minimum, pinned with `.activityTempo` / `runAtActivityTempo()`), a frame only while something moves, pause with no window, off screen, occluded, inactive. Dive and scrub may use the display's rate.
- Only one brain is open per window; textures are shared per process and live while any brain is open.
- Nothing is invented: wires are sequence, calls of one message and literal path or command ties; replay uses message times; provenance is literal.
- No code comments except `///` doc comments, no MARK, no file headers (repo rule). Strings through `Localized.text`. Swift 6 strict concurrency, Core types `Sendable` and never `@MainActor`.

## Core: `TailscodeCore/Sources/TailscodeCore/Neural/`

Design space is 640 by 420, y down. Zone centres: think (456,150), act (348,104), sense (204,162), shell (240,284), delegate (396,250), say (520,306). The usable slots of a zone are the hex-spiral positions (spacing 28, say 24; 19 candidates, say 7) whose node (radius 10) lies inside the dome outline (the jaw ellipse for say); the capacities are think 19, sense 19, act 16, shell 12, delegate 13, say 7 and a test asserts them. The dome outline is `M128,212 C112,132 176,72 268,54 C366,38 462,52 512,108 C540,142 534,196 512,234 C494,260 452,268 420,270 L334,298 C296,314 254,322 214,314 C168,302 134,266 128,212 Z` sampled in 24 steps per curve; the jaw is the ellipse centre (520,306) radii 58 and 40.

### The turn partition (all clients use it)

A turn is the run of assistant messages between two user messages (or the end). Walk its parts in order. A `.reasoning` part with words is a thinking step. A `.tool` part is a step in the zone of its `summaryKind` (fileRead, fileSearch, webSearch, webFetch: sense; fileEdit, fileWrite: act; shell: shell; subagent, workflow, skill: delegate; taskTracking and question: think; other: sense); tool kinds that already draw a card (subagent, workflow, design board, task board, question) are steps with `isCard` true, and the client still emits their card row. A `.text` part before the last tool or reasoning part is a note (a say step). The trailing `.text` parts after the last tool or reasoning part are the answer, and are not steps. While the turn runs the trailing text is the answer in progress; if a tool or reasoning part later follows, it becomes a note. `.file`, `.compaction`, `.note` and unknown parts are never steps. `memberPartIDs` lists the parts the brain consumes (reasoning, plain tools, notes); a card tool's part is a member only for the node, its card row is still built. A tool part's status maps: pending and running to running (or waiting when an approval is pending for it, or answer for a question awaiting), completed to ok, error to failed, stopped to stopped; a running tool the machine reports stuck is stalled. The turn's outcome: running while `status == .running`; failed when its last message has an error; stopped, interrupted and answerless as the Kit reports them; otherwise settled.

## Assets

`scripts/neural-head/render.sh` renders the layers; `scripts/neural-head/pack.py` (new) turns them into `Resources/Neural/`: the packed and resized set (chassis, interior, glass at half resolution, three RGBA masks of 12 channels without the visor backing, three bloom textures at 1/8) as ASTC (chassis 4×4, the rest 6×6, with mips, in a small container: magic, format, size, mip lengths, bytes) for the Apple clients and as RGBA8 PNG for the CPU compositor and tests. iOS bundles the ASTC set; the Mac bundles it too (an Intel Mac without ASTC keeps the Lines style and the glyph); Linux installs the PNG set to `share/tailscode/neural/` (and finds `Resources/Neural/` beside a development checkout, or `TAILSCODE_NEURAL_DIR`). Core never calls `Bundle.module`.

## Renderers

- Apple (iOS and Mac), shared source in `Shared/Neural/` added to both targets in `project.yml`: `NeuralMetalRenderer` (two pipelines from `NeuralShaders.metal`, function constants for the light-theme spill, ripple, sweep, crunch and rainbow, the dome drawn as a triangle fan from `NeuralDome.triangleFan`, sRGB views for the colour layers, ASTC with mips, a three-buffer uniform ring filled from `NeuralFrame`, no per-frame allocation), a `CAMetalLayer` or `MTKView` that follows the orb's lifecycle (`isPaused` when settled, no window, off screen, occluded or inactive) at `.activityTempo`, `maximumDrawableCount = 2`. The interior is an opaque layer, the front (chassis, glass, the eye, ear and ribs glow) a transparent premultiplied one, the nodes layers between, exactly as the browser mock stacks them.
- Linux: `NeuralCompositor` into three overlaid drawing areas (interior, nodes, front); the tick at the activity tempo through the existing tick helper; a frame only when `NeuralFrame.settled` is false or the hover, theme, size or dive changes.
- Nodes (all clients): a shape per zone (circle with a dashed halo, diamond, chipped square, hexagon, octagon with an inner ring, speech bubble), numbered, tone from the status (waiting and stalled attention, failed danger, running live), the waiting node's heartbeat and the running node's breath animated by the render server on Apple (`CAKeyframeAnimation` with `runAtActivityTempo()`) and by the tick on Linux; hover or focus lights the node's wires and shows its card, a click pins it; past 60 nodes the Apple nodes are drawn as instanced SDF shapes in the same pass instead of layers.

## Chat integration (per client)

Each client has its own row builder; each emits one new row per turn, `brain:<turn id>`, at the position of the turn's first member part, and skips the member parts' own rows. The row is a collapsed line (the 28 pt glyph, the count, the mix and duration, `+N −M`, a Rows pill) or, when open, the line plus the stage (the layers and nodes), the one-line ticker, and the scrubber once settled. The latest turn is open by default and stays open until a newer turn starts or the person closes it; opening another closes the first. Rows lists the old step rows inline. The header's status line, the dock, the composer and the approval and question cards are untouched. In-conversation find searches a brain's steps; accessibility reads `NeuralReading.spoken` and exposes each step; reduced motion holds every state still.

- iOS: `ChatRowBuilder.makeRows`, `ChatRow.Content` (a new `brain` case and its arms in `makeCell`, `ChatSpacing.rowClass(of:)` and `ChatFind.searchText`), a cell that hosts the process's one brain host view only while open, expansion in `expandedReasoning`-style state keyed by row id with the scroll anchor held (`revealToggledRow`), the live row's cascade untouched.
- Mac: `TranscriptRowBuilder` (a `Kind.brain`), `RowHost` sizing, `PaneLifetime` releasing the stage when the pane parks, `ChatChecks` additions, `--tree` entries.
- Linux: `TranscriptRow.Kind.brain`, `TranscriptRow.makeWidget`, the row memo key, `TAILSCODE_DRIVE` verbs (`brain=open|close|state=<id>|theme=<id>`), `SelfTest` checks, screenshots through `scripts/dev-linuxapp.sh shot`.

## Away and sharing

- Live Activity: the head glyph (44 pt on the Lock Screen, 38 pt expanded) replaces `PhaseIcon`'s symbol; compact and minimal stay symbols. Notification long look and `NoticeFace`: the head at 34 to 46 pt in the tone tile. Both draw `NeuralGlyph`.
- Share a turn: a poster painted from `NeuralShare` with the hero from `NeuralCompositor` (iOS and Mac through their existing card renderers, Linux through `ShareCardDialog`).
- Haptics: node fire plays `.step`, a waiting node `.needsYou`, settling `.received`.

## Existing code to improve

The audit of the three clients (clocks that ignore settled, hidden or occluded; per-frame allocation; the orb's CPU cost; work per streamed token) is applied in the same change; its findings and what was done are listed at the end of `docs/neural-trace-performance.md`.

## Gates

Core: `cd TailscodeCore && swift test` passes (new Neural tests included), `scripts/parity.sh --check` passes, `scripts/l10n-check.py --strict` passes. Linux: `swift build` and `--selftest` pass headless, shots of every state and theme through the harness, the resident set stable over a two-minute live turn, CPU time per frame recorded. Mac: `scripts/build-mac.sh` compiles, `--selftest` and the chat checks pass, shots through the macbook. iOS: the simulator build compiles and runs the demo chat. Installs follow the repo's rules (`scripts/install-linuxapp.sh`, `scripts/install-macapp.sh`; iOS is the person's to install).
